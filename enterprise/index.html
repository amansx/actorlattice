Establish the boundaries before the pilot

Business automation touches records, people and outside systems. An enterprise evaluation should make those boundaries explicit before sensitive work enters the process.

Data and deployment

Agree what data the workflow needs, where it will live and which external services may receive it. Discuss deployment requirements, retention and the systems that remain authoritative. Do not assume a diagram settles those choices.

For a first legal pilot, map the client and matter identifiers, document location, source of billing data and record owner for each update. Decide whether the trial uses representative data or real client information. These choices determine what the application may display and what an actor may send outside the firm.

Permissions and human oversight

Define who can read, change, execute and approve. Give digital workers a scoped responsibility. Keep consequential actions behind the firm's required review and make exceptions visible to the people responsible.

Ask how a temporary assignment ends, how access is revoked and what happens if approval is still pending when the responsible person changes. A pilot should demonstrate a negative case as well as the intended happy path: a worker without the right relationship must be unable to proceed.

Operational ownership

Agree what happens when a service is unavailable, a document is incomplete or the process stops mid-step. A useful evaluation should demonstrate recovery and ownership, not only a successful happy-path demo.

An outside service failure should be visible as a pending handoff with a clear owner. Teams should be able to tell whether a message was merely prepared, actually sent or still waiting. Agree which events are recorded, who can inspect them and what an operator may safely retry.

Start with an inspectable workflow

Legal onboarding gives the first evaluation a concrete boundary. Identify the baseline, permitted actions, integrations and acceptance criteria, then review the result before expanding to another process.

Law-firm workflow · Integration planning

Confirm the requirements of your deployment

Availability, support, data handling and security requirements must be agreed for the evaluated product and deployment. This page makes no claim of certification, regulatory compliance, a production SLA or a deployment guarantee.

Bring a small set of acceptance scenarios to the evaluation: one ordinary onboarding, one missing document, one rejected permission, one human approval and one unavailable outside service. Define the observed result that would make each scenario acceptable to the firm.

Questions procurement and operations teams ask

Can the firm choose where its data is hosted?

Deployment options are part of the evaluated offer. Agree the actual topology, data location, backups, access and support responsibilities in writing before real client information is used. The website does not promise every hosting model.

Does ActorLattice certify compliance for the firm?

No. The firm must assess its obligations and the controls of the particular release and deployment. Product architecture diagrams do not establish a certification or a legal conclusion.

How are failed handoffs handled?

The evaluation should show how an interrupted step is reported, what was confirmed, who owns recovery and whether a retry is safe. Exact behavior depends on the connected service and the implemented workflow.
