Give onboarding a process your team can follow

After a client signs, the administrative work begins: request documents, check what arrived, update records and tell the next person what is ready. When that work is spread across WhatsApp, email and spreadsheets, your team becomes the coordination system.

ActorLattice is being developed to make that workflow explicit, starting with Brazilian law firms.

From signed client to ready matter

  1. Client signs. Start the agreed onboarding process with the information the firm already has.

  2. Request documents. Identify what this matter needs and prepare the defined requests and follow-ups.

  3. Check what arrived. Make missing or ambiguous information visible. Use interpretation assistance where appropriate; route uncertainty to a person.

  4. Update the matter. Keep records and onboarding status together so the team can see what is ready.

  5. Assign the work. Hand the next task to the responsible person with the context they need.

  6. Prepare the billing handoff. Apply the firm's rules and required approvals before triggering an agreed outside service.

A closer look at the document step

A firm chooses the checklist for a type of matter. When a document arrives, the process records its source and status. A routine completeness check can identify a missing item; an ambiguous file goes to the responsible person. That reviewer sees the file, the question and the next action it would unlock. A “received” label does not imply the document has been accepted or interpreted correctly.

A closer look at the handoff

Once the required information is approved, the next task can carry the matter context to its assigned owner. A billing action is a separate decision with its own permission and confirmation. This keeps an incomplete onboarding from silently becoming an apparently ready matter.

Your firm's judgment stays with your firm

The purpose is operational coordination, not replacing legal judgment. Define which steps are routine, which require an authorized person and what information an external service may receive. Do not send client files through an evaluation form.

Validate a real workflow before expanding it

Our first design partner is validating onboarding. The evaluation should establish the current handoffs, missing-document follow-ups and time to a ready matter, then compare the agreed process against that baseline. We do not yet publish measured savings or a production outcome for that work.

Questions law firms ask

Will ActorLattice decide legal questions for our lawyers?

The first workflow concerns operational onboarding. AI may assist with a bounded interpretation task, but the firm's lawyers retain legal judgment. Define any proposed AI step, its permitted sources and its review requirement before a pilot.

Do we need to replace our matter system?

The pilot should identify which system owns each record. ActorLattice can be evaluated around a defined handoff while an existing matter or billing system remains authoritative; the exact integration must be confirmed.

How would we know the pilot helped?

Agree a baseline and measurement window first. Compare missing-document follow-ups, staff coordination effort and time to a ready matter for the scoped workflow. Do not treat an investor target as an observed result.
