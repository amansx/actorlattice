Build around an outcome

Most business processes need more than a chat window. They need records that stay current, steps that know what comes next and a person who can resolve an exception.

ActorLattice brings those concerns into one product direction: an application people can use and digital workers that act within its rules.

For a legal onboarding process, that means the firm can see what it expects from a new client, what has arrived, who must review it and what may happen after approval. The platform should carry that context from the first request through the final handoff instead of making staff reconstruct it across messages and spreadsheets.

Sentinel

Application and operating foundation.

Bring together the application experience, capabilities and operating tools. Sentinel's existing foundations include a Go/Fx runtime, desktop application shell, browser automation and packaging. The wider integrated platform is still developing.

ActorDB

The active-data design connects records, actors and durable process state. Instead of treating a record update as the end of a task, it can become the input to the next defined step.

Spinoza

Define whether an action is allowed before it changes business state. Permissions belong alongside the work, including the actions taken by digital workers.

Follow one change through the system

A new document arrives. ActorDB's proposed active-data layer records its relationship to the matter and makes the next defined task visible. Spinoza's authorization design determines which actor may read it and whether a person must approve the result. Sentinel delivers the interface where staff can review the exception and carries an approved outside action when the process calls for one. One step should have one accountable owner even though several platform parts contribute.

Questions teams ask

Do we have to adopt every product at once?

Begin with a bounded workflow and the capabilities it actually needs. The evaluation should state which ActorLattice components are available for that workflow and what existing systems continue to own.

Where do outside services fit?

Email, documents, billing and other services are called by defined process steps. The specific integration, permission, expected result and failure behavior must be agreed for the evaluated scope.

What does a human approve?

The firm defines consequential actions and exceptions. A person may review an ambiguous document, authorize a message or approve a billing handoff. That boundary should be visible in the application, not hidden in a prompt.
