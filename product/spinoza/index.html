Spinoza: define who may act before the action happens

Spinoza describes the authorization boundary for people, actors and business actions in ActorLattice.

Digital workers need boundaries too

Permission to read a matter is not permission to approve it. Permission to prepare a message is not permission to send it. Spinoza's role in the ActorLattice design is to make those distinctions part of the action path.

Govern people and actors

Use a common authorization model to describe which people and digital workers may perform a particular action with particular information. Consequential work remains subject to the authority the business has granted.

The relationship matters more than the job title

A lawyer assigned to a matter may need its documents; a billing colleague may need only the approved billing information. A document actor may need to inspect one uploaded file to identify what is missing. None of those relationships grants unrestricted access to every client or every action. Spinoza's proposed model describes the person or actor, the resource, the permitted action and the business context in which the permission applies.

Example actionBoundary the firm should define
Open a matterWhich team member is assigned, and which fields may they see?
Summarize a documentWhich actor may read the file, and who may see the summary?
Change onboarding statusWhat evidence and role allow this change?
Send a follow-upMay a worker send it, or must a person approve it?
Trigger billingWhich approval is required before calling an outside service?

This is an evaluation checklist for the first workflow, not a claim that every control in the table is already shipped.

Put review where it belongs

An incomplete onboarding file may need staff review before work continues. A billing handoff may need approval before an outside service is called. Define those boundaries in the workflow rather than hoping an agent remembers them.

The reviewer needs to see the matter, the proposed action and the reason the system paused. After a decision, the workflow should check current permission again before continuing; a previous grant should not silently justify a later action after a person's assignment changes.

Control information throughout an AI task

An AI-assisted step can read a source, call a tool and produce a derived value. Spinoza's design asks which source fields the step may use, whether an output reveals restricted facts and which audience may later see it. A useful summary should not become a back door to information the requesting person could not read directly. The same question applies to a screen, a live update and an external action.

The intended record of an important decision should explain who or what requested the action, which relationship authorized it, what approval was needed and what finally happened. That is a design goal for inspectable work, not a claim of a current audit product or certification.

ActorDB describes records, actors and durable next actions. Spinoza supplies the authorization decision for proposed reads or changes. Sentinel delivers the application and can carry an approved local or external capability request. The first evaluation should identify which of those boundaries is present in the actual release, then test it with a real onboarding exception.

Questions teams ask

Is Spinoza a replacement for our identity provider?

The design addresses permission within ActorLattice applications: what an identified person or actor may do with a particular resource. An existing identity provider can still establish who a person is. Integration details depend on the evaluated deployment.

Can a digital worker approve its own work?

That depends on the firm's policy for the action. A routine change may be permitted within a narrow scope; a consequential handoff can require a person assigned to the matter. The exact approval classes must be confirmed for the evaluated release.

Does this make a law firm compliant by itself?

No. The firm must assess its actual deployment, data handling, operating controls and legal obligations. Spinoza is an authorization architecture; it does not substitute for that assessment.
