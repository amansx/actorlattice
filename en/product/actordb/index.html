ActorDB: connect the record to the next step

ActorDB is ActorLattice's active-data and durable-work design, connecting business records with scoped actors.

Business records are part of a process

A document received, a task completed or an approval granted changes what should happen next. ActorDB's design places business data and the work around it in the same operating model.

See how the platform works

Keep the process state with the work

The intended runtime connects records, actors and durable execution state. A worker acts on a defined responsibility, records its outcome and leaves the process ready for the next permitted action.

For a law firm, this means a received document and the work it creates can be considered together. A file's arrival may make a review task ready. Missing information may keep the next handoff waiting. An approved review may release a defined task. The matter view should show those differences rather than just a collection of files.

Focused actors with a visible result

An actor has a bounded input and responsibility. One may check completeness; another may prepare a follow-up; another may surface a summary for a reviewer. The intended cycle begins when a relevant business event occurs, wakes the responsible actor, validates its proposal and records the resulting state. A person remains responsible for decisions that require judgment.

Use AI for a bounded task

AI assistance should have a defined input and an expected result. Document interpretation can help a process move forward without making the model responsible for every record update or approval.

The proposed task contract includes which records may be supplied, what result is expected, what validation applies and what evidence should accompany the proposal. If an unfamiliar file produces an uncertain classification, the process can ask a person instead of treating the model output as an approved legal finding.

Make the handoff explicit

In legal onboarding, the useful question is not only “Do we have the document?” It is also “Has it been reviewed, what is missing and who owns the next step?” The data model and workflow need to answer together.

Record the decision before an outside action

Email, billing and document services can be slow or unavailable. ActorDB's design records an authorized work item before a runner performs an outside effect. That item retains the business reason, permitted capability, approval and expected result. If the outside step fails, the application should show what remains pending and who can resolve it.

An attempted document request is different from a confirmed request. A matter must not appear fully onboarded merely because a call was started. This distinction makes the business state useful when the outside world fails.

Spinoza determines whether a person or actor may use the record or perform the action. Sentinel delivers the application and operating capability. Keeping those responsibilities distinct lets a firm evaluate the actual product boundary instead of assuming an illustration is proof of a completed deployment.

Product direction, clearly labeled

ActorDB is presented here as the platform's active-data architecture and developing product direction. This page does not claim general availability, production durability guarantees or measured performance. Those require implementation evidence for the evaluated release.

Questions teams ask

Is ActorDB replacing our current database?

The direction is to own active records and work needed by an ActorLattice application. Existing client, document or billing systems may remain authoritative. The first evaluation should identify the owner of each record instead of assuming a wholesale replacement.

What happens when an AI step is uncertain?

The proposed task contract can require validation or human review before a result changes business state. The process should show the pending question and its owner. Exact thresholds and review rules are part of the evaluated workflow.

Can a failed outside action be retried safely?

The architecture separates a recorded decision from the outside effect so a failure can be identified and handled. Actual retry guarantees depend on the implemented release and connected service; this page does not promise that every action is reversible.
